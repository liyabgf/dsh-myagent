Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways

Google DeepMind safety researcher Bilal Chughtai resigned, warning AI could kill humanity.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei urged slower AI progress, stronger safety testing, and international cooperation.

OpenAI’s Sam Altman and Elon Musk publicly endorsed Amodei’s call to pace frontier AI development.

Bilal Chughtai, a research engineer who worked on AGI safety and alignment at Google DeepMind, resigned in July 2026 and posted a stark warning on both X and LinkedIn. “AI has the potential to kill us all, and that we might be running out of time to avoid this outcome,” he wrote, according to Reuters.

In posts reported by Bloomberg, Chughtai said he was “extremely concerned by the default trajectory” of AI development after witnessing it firsthand at Google DeepMind. His work in safety and alignment research, the field focused on keeping AI-powered systems controllable and within human intent, makes the departure pointed rather than peripheral.

A Pattern of Internal Dissent

Chughtai’s resignation is part of a documented pattern of safety staff departing frontier AI labs.

Beyond Chughtai, Josh Engels, a separate former Google DeepMind AGI safety researcher, also left the company and joined METR, per NDTV reporting.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei added institutional weight to these concerns through an essay titled “We Must Pace the Frontier.” He argued that AI progress has accelerated “drastically faster” and called for stronger safety testing, independent evaluations, and international cooperation, according to Reuters and the BBC.

Rare Consensus at the Top

Rival AI lab leaders have publicly aligned on the need to slow frontier development, a striking convergence across fiercely competitive organizations.

Sam Altman said, “I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier,” and Elon Musk responded simply, “Dario is right,” both per Reuters.

This is no longer a theoretical argument confined to academic papers. When safety researchers resign over existential concerns and the chief executives of Anthropic, OpenAI, and other major players publicly endorse slowing development, the conversation has moved from speculative risk to institutional reckoning. That shift is now on the record, in resignations and executive statements alike, even as the societal costs of expansion , from AI data centers to workforce displacement , continue to mount.
