I. INTRODUCTION  
Pursuant to Sections V and VI.B of the Final Judgment in the above-captioned cases, 
Plaintiffs and the Technical Committee (“TC”), with input from Google, are required to create 
and submit to the Court templates for a search syndication license and a Search Text Ads 
syndication license. ECF No. 1462 at §§ V , VI.B [hereinafter Final J.]. Search syndication is 
critical to the success of the Court’s remedies: Because “[i]t will take time for a Qualified 
Competitor to build its own search index and the capacity to deliver high-quality search results,” 
Qualified Competitors need remedial search syndication to compete in the short term, 
particularly when answering long-tail, local, and fresh queries. ECF No. 1435 at 170–71 
[hereinafter Rem. Op.]. Search Text Ads syndication is similarly important, as it enables 
Qualified Competitors to monetize high-quality ads from the start. Id. at 183. Under the Court’s 
Minute Order of October 6, 2026, the template licenses are to be submitted to the Court by 
October 9, 2026.

Attachment 1 hereto, that provides license terms for both search syndication and Search Text Ads 
syndication (“Recommended Template”).1 The Recommended Template is the culmination of

III. RECOMMENDATION STATEMENT 
Plaintiffs, together with the TC, recommend that the Court adopt the Recommended 
Template, which is incorporated by reference into this Recommendation Statement, in 
satisfaction of the Final Judgment’s requirement to create and submit template search syndication 
and Search Text Ads syndication licenses. Final J. §§ V , VI.B. 
The Recommended Template is designed as a single, modular syndication license 
template that addresses both search syndication and Search Text Ads syndication remedies under 
Sections V and VI.B of the Final Judgment. This combined approach is intended to reflect 
Google’s commercial syndication agreements, many of which include both search syndication 
and ads syndication, while preserving necessary flexibility for Qualified Competitors (as that 
term is defined in Section IX.V of the Final Judgment). Under the proposed template, for 
example, Qualified Competitors may elect to receive all remedial syndication services or only a 
subset, with terms reflecting the specific syndication services a Qualified Competitor selects. See 
Attach. 1, Ex. B.  
The Recommended Template is further intended to maximize the operational efficiency 
of implementing these licenses. It is drafted to apply equally to any Qualified Competitor, 
including providers of General Search Engines, Search Text Ads, or GenAI Products. Similarly, 
the Recommended Template is drafted to effectuate processes related to Qualified Competitor 
certification, enforcement, and compliance monitoring without needing to amend or modify the 
template on an ongoing basis. Beyond the terms in the Recommended Template, Google remains 
entitled to propose additional terms related to Search Text Ads syndication as necessary to 
guarantee ad quality, protect advertisers, and prevent ad misuse, and Qualified Competitors will 
remain entitled to reject these proposed additional terms. See Final J. § VI.B.11. Plaintiffs 
additionally propose that Qualified Competitors may negotiate modified or additional terms to

rejection of any terms proposed by Qualified Competitors does not violate the Final Judgment, 
including the requirement that Google provide syndication licenses to Qualified Competitors on

Under the Final Judgment, the template syndication license must include various terms to 
foster competition by Qualified Competitors, including terms setting out, e.g.: (1) the pricing and 
methods for delivering syndication services to Qualified Competitors; (2) the latency, reliability, 
and performance of syndication made available to Qualified Competitors; (3) the terms over 
which Qualified Competitors can display, use, and format syndicated search results and Search 
Text Ads; and (4) the information from Qualified Competitors that Google may receive, retain, 
and use while providing remedial syndication services to Qualified Competitors. Final J. §§ V , 
VI.B. Where the Final Judgment sets explicit requirements, those requirements are reflected 
within the main body of the Recommended Template. Where the Final Judgment demands terms 
no less favorable than Google’s commercial agreements—or explicitly pins terms to Google’s 
commercial agreements, see Final J §§ V .B.7, VI.B.6—the Recommended Template is 
supplemented with appropriate language in exhibits derived from the TC’s analysis of Google’s 
commercial syndication agreements, see, e.g., Attach. 1 at Exs. B–C. 
The Recommended Template does not presently include a recommended tapering 
schedule. Under Section V .B.4 of the Final Judgment, Qualified Competitors’ use of Google’s 
search syndication must be capped at 40% of annual U.S. queries for the first year, with

IV. BASIS STATEMENT 
As described below, in developing the Recommended Template, Plaintiffs and the TC 
affirmatively sought input from Google, as required by Sections V and VI.B of the Final 
Judgment. Plaintiffs and the TC also reviewed documents and information provided by Google, 
as well as engaged with third parties. Plaintiffs and the TC further brought to bear the cumulative 
experience of Plaintiffs in the underlying litigation and of TC Members and TC Staff as 
professionals working extensively in the industry and in academia for over 20 years. 
A. Input From Google 
Pursuant to Section VII.A.7.c of the Final Judgment, the TC sent two demand letters to 
Google on issues related to search syndication and Search Text Ads syndication. The TC sent the 
first letter on April 27, 2026, seeking Google’s current syndication agreements and 
documentation of its current syndication services and partners (“Demand Letter #1”). The TC 
also invited any other input or information that Google wished to provide regarding syndication 
license terms. In response, Google made a partial production to the TC on May 1, 2026, followed 
by another partial production on May 8, 2026.

recommendation statement, basis statement, and template license to Google. On September 8, 
2026, Google provided a written set of requests for additional information, and Plaintiffs 
provided responses and additional information to Google on September 15, 2026. In addition, as 
discussed above, Google requested three calls between Google employees and the TC on three 
different categories of topics related to the template license, which calls were held on September 
25 and September 29, 2026. After receiving Google’s objections on September 29, 2026, the 
Parties also held two meet and confers.  
Plaintiffs and the TC developed the Recommended Template based on the syndication 
agreements Google produced, together with the TC’s analysis of the most favorable terms across 
all available agreements. That analysis identified the best available commercial terms for each 
provision, from which Plaintiffs and the TC derived terms consistent with this Court’s instruction 
that, with few exceptions, the Recommended Template include terms “no worse than” or 
“functionally equivalent to” those offered to any other user of Google’s search syndication and 
Search Text Ads products. See, e.g., Final J. §§ V .B.2–3, VI.B.1–2; Final J. Op. at 37–44. In 
addition, Plaintiffs and the TC relied on information from third parties, the TC’s cumulative
