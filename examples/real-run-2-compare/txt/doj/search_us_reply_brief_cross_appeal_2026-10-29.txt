INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT 
Google does not dispute the key legal principles governing this 
cross-appeal: Relief in government antitrust enforcement actions must 
effectively redress the violation and restore competition in the relevant 
market(s). Google.Third.Br.65-66, 69. A remedy’s effectiveness depends 
on whether it restores the competitive process in the relevant market(s), 
not its short-term market outcomes. Google.Third.Br.86. And courts 
may not forgo an effective remedy because of harm to private interests. 
Google.Third.Br.69. 
Instead, Google relies on findings that the district court never 
made: Google claims the court found that its remedy would be effective 
and that a payment ban would likely harm competition and consumers. 
But those findings are not in the remedies opinion. The court cited 
United States v. E.I. du Pont de Nemours & Co., 366 U.S. 316, 326-27 
(1961) (requring effective relief), and it hoped that a remedy without a 
payment ban would effectively restore competition. But the court made 
no finding that competition would likely be restored. To the contrary, 
the court expressed serious unresolved doubts about whether 
competition would return without a payment ban. And the possible

Because the court’s analysis did not “apply the correct legal

I. Google Misdescribes The District Court’s Factual Findings 
And The Record. 
A. The Court Did Not Find that a Remedy Without a 
Payment Ban Would Be Effective.

“reasonably assure[d]” that competition would be restored before 
considering private interests. 366 U.S. at 326-27. But Google cannot 
point to an express effectiveness finding, and the court’s isolated 
comments on other remedies do not add up to one. 
1. The court did not expressly find the remedy 
effective. 
a. The court never stated the remedy would be effective 
without a payment ban. To the contrary, the court expressly declined to 
make such a finding, citing Ginsburg v. InBev NV/SA, 623 F.3d 1229, 
1235 (8th Cir. 2010), for the proposition that it should “balance the

Decision. 
This Court should vacate and remand for the district court to 
determine, under the correct legal standard, whether a payment ban is 
necessary for effective relief. This Court should make clear that the 
district court must make that determination before any efforts to 
mitigate harm to private interests or effects in other markets. If the
