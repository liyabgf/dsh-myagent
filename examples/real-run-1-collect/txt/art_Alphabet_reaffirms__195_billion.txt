Google's parent is holding its raised 2026 spending plan even as free cash flow turns negative and cloud revenue more than doubles

by
Diego Almada Lopez

Wed 28 Oct 2026 · 17:14 EDT
·
2 min read

In Technology

Share

Follow on Google

Alphabet still expects to spend $195 billion to $205 billion on capital expenditures this fiscal year. The company first raised that range in July, and it held firm after its latest results.

The numbers behind the plan

Alphabet lifted its fiscal 2026 capex outlook during its second-quarter earnings call on July 22/23, 2026. The previous range was $180 billion to $190 billion, so the midpoint jumped by $15 billion in a single update.

When the company reported third-quarter results on October 28, 2026, it reaffirmed the higher range. Q3 capex came in at $51.36 billion.

That followed $44.9 billion in Q2, which was nearly double what Alphabet spent in the same quarter a year earlier.

Most of the money is going into hardware. About 60% of the planned spending is earmarked for servers and AI accelerators, the specialized chips that train and run models like Gemini.

Cloud growth is doing the heavy lifting

Google Cloud’s revenue grew 82% year-over-year to $24.8 billion in Q2. In Q3, cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.71 billion, meaning the business more than doubled compared with a year earlier.

Google Cloud’s backlog now exceeds $500 billion, representing contracted work that has yet to be recognized as revenue.

The cost: negative free cash flow

Free cash flow turned negative by $5.9 billion in Q2. It worsened in Q3 to a $9.07 billion outflow. Alphabet posting negative free cash flow in back-to-back quarters marks the first time that has happened in nearly two decades.

Alphabet is not short on revenue. It is simply choosing to plow more than it earns back into infrastructure for now.

Background: an AI arms race measured in billions

The July guidance raise was a turning point in tone. Rather than signaling caution, Alphabet told investors it would accelerate, and the October reaffirmation confirmed it was not a one-off gesture.

What this means

Watch three things in the quarters ahead. First, whether cloud revenue growth holds near triple digits; second, how quickly backlog converts into recognized revenue; and third, when free cash flow turns positive again.

Pichai’s comment that compute demand is outrunning supply cuts both ways. It explains the spending, but it also means Alphabet’s growth is partly capped by how fast it can build.
