Learjet parts found

Schools that want to block Google's AI tools from student accounts will also lose access to Google Search on those accounts. That condition sits at the center of a new K–12 policy Google confirmed for rollout in early 2027, and it has drawn sharp criticism from elected officials, a former federal regulator, and education advocates.

The Policy Details

Google will turn off four products by default on school-managed accounts until administrators explicitly enable each one.

Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will all be disabled by default on K–12 school-managed accounts under the new framework. Google says the change responds to feedback from schools, teachers, researchers, and policymakers who asked for stronger controls over how AI reaches students.

The Search Condition

The complication is a bundling decision that administrators cannot work around.

Schools that disable AI tools entirely will also lose Google Search for students on managed accounts; those students will instead see a message indicating that Search is unavailable, according to Google's education chief, Chris Phillips, speaking to The New York Times. Google is not offering a fully non-AI Search option under the new system. Phillips told the Times that Search and AI are now "deeply integrated," making separation a policy outcome rather than a technical option available to schools.

Guided Learning, Defined on Google's Terms

Google says a new tutoring feature coaches students through problems rather than supplying direct answers.

Alongside the policy shift, Google is expanding a feature called Guided Learning, which uses open-ended questions and incremental explanations to walk students through problem-solving. Google describes the approach as fostering "productive struggle," meaning students work through difficulty in ways intended to produce deeper learning. The feature is available in Google Classroom and will be tested inside Google Search at selected U.S. schools before broader expansion.

The Political Response

New York City's mayor accused Google of using market power to impose AI on public school students.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani did not soften his criticism, saying in remarks posted on X and reported by Yahoo Finance: "Google is trying to use its monopoly power to force AI into our public schools." He added that no corporation should dictate how children are taught.

Antitrust and Labor Voices

A former federal regulator and a major union leader each argued the policy puts business interests ahead of students.

Former Federal Trade Commission Chair Lina Khan characterized the policy as Google using its search position to pressure schools into adopting AI, according to CNBC. Randi Weingarten, president of the American Federation of Teachers, said Google had prioritized business interests over children's education. She called on the company to work with teachers and parents on privacy and safety rather than present an all-or-nothing condition.

An Open Question on Evidence

Advocates say schools have alternatives, while research on AI's classroom benefits remains contested.

Josh Golin, executive director of Fairplay, argued that schools should consider privacy-protective search alternatives rather than treat Google Search as indispensable. That point may carry additional weight given the state of the underlying research: NBC News reporting found that evidence for AI's educational benefits remains limited, and that some studies claiming substantial learning gains have faced criticism over their methodology.
